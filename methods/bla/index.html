Best linear approximation per burst¶

STFT reads the excited bins of each window and divides. That gives an impedance and nothing else — a single window offers no way to say whether the number it produced is limited by measurement noise, by nonlinearity, or by the cell changing while the window was open.

This page is about recovering that. It keeps STFT's assumption — the system is whatever it is inside one window, with no model of how it varies — and uses the bins STFT throws away.

The one structural fact¶

Cut the record into windows of \(P\) whole multisine periods. The DFT of a window has fundamental \(1/(PT_p)\), so a tone at harmonic \(h\) lands on bin \(Ph\) and only every \(P\)-th bin is a harmonic of the multisine at all. A nonlinear system of the PISPO class (period in, same period out) answers a periodic input with a periodic output, so its distortion is harmonically related to the input and can land only on those bins.

That splits one burst into four classes:

bins what is there
\(k = Ph\), \(h\) excited signal, plus everything below
\(k = Pm\), \(m\) odd, unexcited odd-order distortion + noise + drift
\(k = Pm\), \(m\) even, unexcited even-order distortion + noise + drift
\(k\) not a multiple of \(P\) noise + drift

This needs \(P \ge 2\), and that is the whole price

At \(P = 1\) the window fundamental is the multisine fundamental, every bin is a harmonic, and the fourth class is empty. On the package's 13-tone grid, in band:

\(P\) noise odd even excited
1 0 168 180 13
2 301 138 150 13
4 903 138 150 13
8 2107 138 150 13

One period is exactly the window plain STFT prefers on a strongly excited cell. So the uncertainty is bought with time resolution, and there is no way around it. classify_bins raises rather than returning an empty class.

Calling it¶

from deistools.processing import MultiFrequencyAnalysis

mfa = MultiFrequencyAnalysis(frequencies, voltage, current, sampling_time)
results, t = mfa.run_bla(period=Tp, periods_per_window=4)

for burst in results:
    burst.impedance          # what STFT would have returned
    burst.std_noise          # level at the non-harmonic bins
    burst.std_odd_lines      # level at the unexcited odd harmonics
    burst.std_even_lines     # level at the unexcited even harmonics
    burst.r_ntv              # noise, or the cell moving?
    burst.distortion_odd()   # the subtraction, where it is significant

Or directly, with burst_bla for a single burst and bla_eis to slide.

The noise level is real¶

std_noise is the standard deviation of the impedance, not something proportional to it. Checked against the scatter of \(Z\) over 60 independent noise realisations of a stationary cell:

injected noise predicted / observed
\(10^{-4}\) V 0.997
\(10^{-3}\) V 0.997
\(10^{-2}\) V 0.997

Two decades of noise, the same ratio — so it is calibrated, not merely correlated.

Noise, or the cell moving?¶

The two are told apart by their correlation over frequency, not their size. Stationary noise is uncorrelated between neighbouring DFT bins. A time variation is not: it reaches each bin by convolving the excited lines with the spectrum of the variation, so neighbours share contributions.

For a linear-in-time variation the ratio of the autocorrelation at lags 0 and 1 works out to exactly \(\pi^2/6\) (Lataire 2012, eq. 31), so

\[ R_{\mathrm{NTV}} = \frac{6}{\pi^2}\, \frac{\overline{|V(k)|^2}}{\left|\overline{V(k)\,V^*(k{+}1)}\right|} \]

puts "pure time variation" at 1. Measured on 80 s bursts:

record \(R_{\mathrm{NTV}}\) verdict
stationary R+RC, noise \(10^{-4}\) V 9.2 – 19.4 noise
block-oriented (nonlinear and varying) 0.97 time variation
transistor (linear, varying) 0.73 time variation

Five consecutive bursts of the transistor record all return 0.73; five of the stationary record return 9.2 to 19.4. The separation is clean and it needs no model.

It needs a residual to work on

On a noiseless simulation the non-harmonic bins hold nothing but round-off and the statistic is meaningless — measured at 0.86 for a stationary noiseless cell, which reads as "time variation" for a system that has none. Real records always have noise; simulated ones should be given some.

What std_noise really contains¶

On a varying cell the non-harmonic bins are not noise alone. Each tone grows a skirt — for a linear-in-time variation \(B_1(k) \sim 1/k\) — and with a dozen tones the skirts cover the band. Measured on a 40 s burst cut from the usual 400 s transistor record, against the pure-noise floor at the same bins:

distance to the nearest tone ratio to the noise floor
1–2 bins 7656
6–10 bins 463
21–50 bins 71
51–100 bins 44

It decays, and it never arrives. So std_noise is an upper bound, and on a strongly varying cell a loose one. That is not an implementation limit: separating the two needs a model of the variation, which is what BLTVA is for and what this page deliberately does not assume. r_ntv tells you which case you are in.

Nonlinear distortion¶

The levels are reported as measured, with nothing subtracted: std_odd_lines and std_even_lines each contain the noise as well as any distortion. The subtraction is available from distortion_odd() and distortion_even(), and returns NaN unless the excess is significant.

That threshold is not decoration. Each level is a mean of n_local bin powers, so it carries about \(1/\sqrt{n_\text{local}}\) relative error and their difference about \(\sqrt{2/n_\text{local}}\). Merely requiring a positive difference fires on 10 of 13 tones of a linear stationary cell, where the two levels agree to 2.5% and the sign is a coin toss. Requiring \(3\sigma\) leaves none.

Measured on a stationary nonlinear cell — one branch, so no time variation — against a linear one:

cell odd / noise even / noise even detected
linear 1.02 0.92 0 / 13
nonlinear, \(a = 0.02\) 1.02 2.65 9 / 13
nonlinear, \(a = 0.1\) 1.02 12.1 9 / 13
nonlinear, \(a = 0.3\) 1.06 30.3 9 / 13

No false alarms on the linear cell, and the level tracks the nonlinearity monotonically over a factor of 15.

Odd-order distortion is the one that matters for an odd multisine: it lands on the excited lines as well as the empty odd ones, so it biases the impedance, where even-order distortion only sits beside it.

On a varying cell the distortion cannot be resolved this way

The non-harmonic bins sit above the harmonic ones, so the subtraction is negative everywhere and every tone returns NaN — measured on the block-oriented system, which is nonlinear and varying, although the distortion is certainly there. Check r_ntv first. If it is near 1, use BLTVA, which models the variation and reports var_distortion_odd with it removed.

Where this sits¶

STFT BLA per burst BLTVA
model of the variation none none Legendre, global
time resolution 1 period \(\ge\) 2 periods \(T/N_p\)
noise level — yes, on a steady cell yes
distortion level — yes, on a steady cell yes, always
works while the cell moves yes the estimate does; the error bars do not yes

The honest summary: this buys STFT an uncertainty on a cell that is nearly steady within a burst, at the cost of halving its time resolution at best. When the cell moves enough that r_ntv falls to 1, the impedance is still STFT's, but the error attribution needs BLTVA.

Sources¶

  • Lataire, Louarroudi and Pintelon, IEEE TIM 61 (2012) 2132 — the best linear time-invariant approximation of a time-varying system, and the frequency-correlation test used here.
  • Pintelon, Louarroudi and Lataire, IEEE TIM 61 (2012) 3387 — the multiple-input time-invariant equivalent, and separating leakage from time variation.
  • Pintelon, Louarroudi and Lataire, IEEE TIM 62 (2013) 3361 — the periodic-excitation procedure this module follows: which bins carry what, and why two periods are the minimum.
  • Pintelon and Schoukens, System Identification: A Frequency Domain Approach, 2nd ed., Wiley-IEEE Press (2012) — the underlying framework.

Not done yet¶

The three-step estimator of Pintelon 2013 §V-B goes further than this page: it models the time variation (fitting the \(H_p\) branches of the multiple-input equivalent at the non-harmonic bins), subtracts it, and then separates noise from distortion at the excited bins. That would lift the restriction above — distortion on a varying cell — and is roadmap item 2b.

One thing blocks a direct transcription: step 3 reconstructs \(G_\text{BLTI}\) from \(H_0\) by central differences over neighbouring excited harmonics, which Assumption 3 of that paper requires to be equispaced. This package's grid is quasi-logarithmic, with a 50x spread in the gaps between neighbouring tones, and on it that differentiation costs 156x on the first derivative while the second-difference formula diverges under refinement.

The implementation note works through why, with the measurements, and gives the replacement: the local polynomial fit already produces the derivatives as its own coefficients, over consecutive DFT bins that are equispaced whatever the tones do.
