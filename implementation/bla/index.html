BLA implementation notes¶

Why step 3 of Pintelon 2013 is not transcribed¶

The per-burst estimator on the BLA page stops short of the full three-step procedure of Pintelon, Louarroudi and Lataire (IEEE TIM 62 (2013) 3361, §V-B). Steps 1 and 2 transcribe directly. Step 3 does not, and the obstruction is this package's frequency grid rather than anything about the method.

This page records why, with the numbers, because it decides how roadmap item 2b has to be built.

Where the derivatives come from¶

The time variation is carried by polynomial basis functions, \(G(s,t) = \sum_p G_p(s) f_p(t)\) with \(f_p\) of degree \(p\). Theorem 2 of the 2012 companion paper rewrites that single-input time-varying system as a multiple-input time-invariant one whose inputs are \(u(t) f_p(t)\).

The bridge is the Laplace duality

\[ \mathcal{L}\{t\,x(t)\} = -X'(s) \]

Multiplying by \(t\) in the time domain is differentiating with respect to \(s\) in the Laplace domain. So trading the \(f_p(t)\) factors for extra inputs turns powers of \(t\) into \(s\)-derivatives, and they survive into the relation between the BLTI approximation and the branch transfer functions (eq. 15):

\[ G_\text{BLTI}(s) = H_0(s) + \frac{2}{T}\sum_{p} H^{(1)}_{2p+1}(s) + \frac{4}{T^2}\sum_{p} \beta_{2p} H^{(2)}_{2p}(s) + O(T^{-3}) \]

Those are derivatives along the frequency axis, of quantities known only as samples, so they have to be computed numerically. Eq. (27) does it with central differences between neighbouring excited harmonics — and that is the step that carries Assumption 3's requirement that the excited harmonics be equispaced.

Two separate things go wrong without it¶

This package's grid is 1, 3, 5, 9, 13, 17, 27, 39, 59, 89, 133, 201, 301, so the gaps between neighbouring excited harmonics are

2  2  4  4  4  10  12  20  30  44  68  100

a 50× spread. Both failure modes below are measured on an R+RC impedance standing in for \(H_p\), whose derivatives are analytic.

The step size¶

A central difference carries \(O(h^2)\) truncation error. Symmetric stencil, widened:

half-span relative error
±2 harmonics 1.18e-04
±4 4.74e-04
±10 2.97e-03
±30 2.69e-02
±100 3.14e-01

2661× over a 50× span, which is \(h^2\) to within the fit. The top of a quasi-log grid sits in the last row.

The asymmetry¶

The \(O(h^2)\) accuracy exists because the odd-order Taylor terms cancel, and that cancellation needs equal distance either side. Unequal spacing puts a term in \(f'\) back. For the first derivative this costs an order of accuracy. For the second derivative — which the \(O(T^{-2})\) term of eq. (15) needs — the three-point formula written for a symmetric stencil is not merely less accurate, it is inconsistent. Fixed 1:2 asymmetry, stencil halved each row:

\(a\) \(b\) formula as written proper unequal-spacing weights
40 80 1.07 1.31e-01
20 40 2.01 8.37e-02
10 20 4.02 4.77e-02
5 10 8.09 2.55e-02
2.5 5 1.62e+01 1.32e-02
1.25 2.5 3.26e+01 6.69e-03

The error doubles every time the stencil is refined. The leading term is \((b-a)f'/h^2 \sim f'/h\), so it diverges as \(h \to 0\): refining the grid makes it worse. The proper weights converge at \(O(h)\), as they should.

At the gap ratios this package's grid actually has:

left right formula as written proper weights
30 30 2.69e-02 2.69e-02
30 44 9.59e-01 4.74e-02
44 68 7.53e-01 7.20e-02
68 100 5.18e-01 1.08e-01

Equal spacing is the only row where the two agree, which is the point. A 30/44 ratio — the gap pattern around harmonic 89 — already gives a 96% error.

What it costs here¶

First derivative, over the whole grid:

grid median error worst
quasi-log (this package) 5.5e-02 8.1e-02 at \(h = 133\)
equispaced, same tone count 3.5e-04 3.6e-04
equispaced, denser 3.1e-04 3.6e-04

156× on the median. Per harmonic:

harmonic stencil span relative error
3 4 3.6e-04
5 6 1.9e-02
9 8 1.4e-03
17 14 5.5e-02
39 32 6.4e-02
89 74 7.2e-02
133 112 8.1e-02
201 168 6.3e-02

It is not monotone, and that is worse than if it were

Harmonic 5 (span 6) is 13× worse than harmonic 9 (span 8), despite the narrower stencil. The step-size error and the asymmetry error have independent signs and partially cancel at some tones. So the damage is unpredictable per tone rather than simply growing with the gap, which rules out correcting it with a rule of thumb.

Since the correction is itself the \(O(T^{-1})\) term, computing it to 5–8% still beats using \(H_0\) alone — but it does not deliver the \(O(T^{-2})\) the construction promises, and the \(O(T^{-2})\) term is unusable outright.

The route that does work¶

Do not difference at all. The local polynomial method already fits a polynomial in the frequency index over a band of consecutive DFT bins,

\[ H(k+r) = \sum_{s=0}^{R} g_s\, r^s \]

and those \(g_s\) are Taylor coefficients. With \(\Delta\omega\) the spacing of one DFT bin,

\[ H' = \frac{g_1}{\Delta\omega}, \qquad H'' = \frac{2 g_2}{\Delta\omega^2} \]

The fit runs over consecutive bins, which are equispaced by construction whatever the tones do, so the grid stops mattering. The \(H_p\) for \(p \ge 1\) are estimated at the non-excited bins in step 1 anyway, so the dense samples are already there.

Recovered from a noiseless fit:

band half-width order \(R\) \(dH/ds\) \(d^2H/ds^2\)
5 2 8.6e-05 1.2e-04
5 3 4.5e-09 1.2e-04
5 4 4.5e-09 8.1e-09
10 3 6.6e-08 4.5e-04
10 4 6.6e-08 1.2e-07
20 4 9.8e-07 1.9e-06

Note the parity: \(R = 3\) fixes the first derivative and leaves the second where \(R = 2\) had it; the second needs \(R = 4\). Adding an odd-order term removes the next even remainder and vice versa. Pintelon 2012 already requires \(R \ge 2\) for this correction, so raising it to 4 is a small change.

That table is noiseless

It isolates the approximation error. With noise, a narrow band and a high order inflate the variance — the usual LPM bias/variance trade, which the LPM's own residuals quantify. The orders above are therefore a starting point, not a recommendation.

This is also why "use adjacent DFT bins in the difference instead" is not the fix it looks like. Adjacent LPM estimates share most of their data, so the difference between them is dominated by correlated estimation error rather than by the derivative. Reading the fitted coefficients avoids that; differencing the fitted outputs does not.

Status¶

Nothing on this page is implemented. It is the design note for roadmap item 2b, recorded now because the measurements were made while deciding whether step 3 could be transcribed as published. It cannot, on this grid, and the replacement is specific enough to build from.

The numbers here are properties of the numerical scheme and the grid, not of package code, so unlike the rest of these notes they are not pinned by tests — there is nothing yet to pin. They are reproducible from the published formulas and the grid in the general convention.
