Implementation notes¶

The papers this package implements leave some things ambiguous, and in a few places this code makes a choice they do not dictate. Those choices change the numbers, so they are recorded here with the evidence rather than left in the source for a reader to reverse-engineer.

This section exists because the package crosses electrochemistry and system theory, where a reader is likely to be expert in one and not the other. If you are going to cite a result from DEIStools, these are the pages to read first.

What is on these pages¶

  • BLTVA — the ambiguous reading of the rank threshold, why the solver deliberately does not equilibrate, the Legendre basis convention, why eq. (49) is necessary but not sufficient, and why \(N_p\) and the band width compete.
  • DMFA — the local filter axis, the edge artefact, and the current verification status.
  • BLA — where the frequency derivatives of eq. (15) come from, and why the central differences of step 3 cannot be transcribed onto a quasi-logarithmic grid: 156× on the first derivative, and a second-difference formula that diverges under refinement. Includes the replacement.

How to read a claim here¶

Every entry states what was tested and what the number was. Where a choice was made between two readings of a paper, both were implemented and measured; the one that is used is the one that was unbiased, not the one that looked plausible.

Where something is not verified, it says so. The DMFA page is mostly such a statement at present, and the BLA page is a design note for work not yet done rather than a record of work finished -- it says so at the end.

A general convention¶

Where a paper gives a rule and this package also allows a manual override, the rule is the default and the override exists because real frequency grids are not always what the papers assumed. Two cases:

quantity paper rule override why the override exists
\(N_a\) eq. (22) of the 2022 paper Na= none needed in practice; the rule beat a hand-picked value by 19×
band width eq. (55) of the 2021 paper band_halfwidth= the rule produces enormous bands at the top of a sparse quasi-log grid

The papers use linearly spaced odd harmonics. Electrochemistry uses logarithmic grids with fifteen tones over three decades. Several rules that are natural on the first are impractical on the second, and where that is the case it is noted on the method's page.

The sharpest instance is on the BLA page: a numerical differentiation that is second-order accurate on an equispaced grid is not merely less accurate on a logarithmic one but, for the second derivative, inconsistent -- its error grows as the grid is refined. The grid this package uses throughout is

1  3  5  9  13  17  27  39  59  89  133  201  301

whose gaps between neighbouring tones span a factor of 50.
